VSEPR THEORY

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory states that electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion, determining the overall 3D molecular shape.

MOLECULAR GEOMETRY

Linear

Electron Domains: 2

Bond Angle: 180°

Example: CO₂, BeCl₂

Straight Line (180°)

Bent

Electron Domains: 3 or 4

Bond Angle: 104.5° (H₂O) / 120° (SO₂)

Example: H₂O, SO₂

Non-linear V-Shape

Trigonal Planar

Electron Domains: 3

Bond Angle: 120°

Example: BF₃, SO₃

Flat Triangle

Tetrahedral

Electron Domains: 4

Bond Angle: 109.5°

Example: CH₄, CCl₄

3D Pyramid with 4 Faces

Trigonal Pyramidal

Electron Domains: 4 (3 bonding, 1 lone pair)

Bond Angle: ~107°

Example: NH₃, PCl₃

Pyramid with Triangular Base

Trigonal Bipyramidal

Electron Domains: 5

Bond Angle: 90° & 120°

Example: PCl₅

Axial & Equatorial Positions

Seesaw

Electron Domains: 5 (4 bonding, 1 lone pair)

Bond Angle: ~90° & ~120°

Example: SF₄

Asymmetric Seesaw Shape

Octahedral

Electron Domains: 6

Bond Angle: 90°

Example: SF₆

Symmetrical 8-Faced Shape

Square Planar

Electron Domains: 6 (4 bonding, 2 lone pairs)

CHEMICAL BONDS

Single Bond (σ)

Formed by the sharing of 1 pair of electrons (2 electrons). Longest bond length and lowest bond dissociation energy.

Double Bond (σ + π)

Formed by sharing 2 pairs of electrons (4 electrons). Stronger and shorter than single bonds.

Triple Bond (σ + 2π)

Formed by sharing 3 pairs of electrons (6 electrons). Shortest and strongest type of covalent bond.
